1A Visual direction — Bank or Bust

Felt & Brass

A dark green felt table lit by brass. The background is the table, the panels are cards laid on it, and the only saturated warm colour in the whole interface is money: the room code, the round pot, the primary button, and locked dice all share one gold. Everything a player can lose is red, everything they are about to choose is a cold blue that cannot be confused with either. The type is one grotesque for voice and one monospace for numbers, so scores, codes and pots read as ledger figures rather than UI labels.

Dice — five states

{{ d.label }} {{ d.spec }}

Body 88×88, radius 16, border 3, pip diameter 12 on a 3×3 grid (5px gutters, 13px inset). The five states differ on two axes at once — body value and border hue — so they survive being read at a glance, at small window sizes, and by colour-blind players: dark-empty, light-neutral, light-with-cold-ring (raised 8px), solid gold, dark-red-crossed.

Layout changes

Columns at 1280: 250 / flexible / 300, 20px gutters, 24px window margin. Below 1000px wide, drop the chat column to a collapsible strip and let the play area take the space; below 760px, stack scoreboard above the play area as a single horizontal row of name+score chips.

The play area now leads with a "Now playing" label and the player's name in their colour, so whose turn it is is legible from across the room. Live dice and round pot become two ledger figures side by side instead of two sentences, with the pot at 56px — it is the number the decision hangs on. The pot turns gold the moment it is non-zero and grey at zero, so "you have something to lose" is a colour change, not a reading task.

Scoreboard rows carry the player colour as a 4px left edge rather than a floating swatch, and the active row is filled and gold-ruled instead of marked with an arrow. Locked dice stay in the row in place, so the row reads left-to-right as history: locked gold, then live blanks.
